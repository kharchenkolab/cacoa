# cacoa (development, branch dev_lm)

## Intervals, small counts, sample diagnostics and grouping terms (2026-11)

- Design check and model report: a continuous covariate whose values in one level of the tested variable lie outside the range of the other level(s) is reported (a note from 10 % of that level's samples, a warning from 25 %), e.g. "16 of 37 'Control' samples have 'age' below the range of 'PF' (43-74)". Adjusted for such a covariate, the tested effect for those samples rests on a fitted trend of the covariate beyond the values observed in the other level; the suggestion is to compare with the samples in the common range (`cao$excludeSamples()`). `checkDesign()` reports it for every continuous covariate, the model report (`setModel()`) for those in the model.
- Sensitivity of a result to the covariate set (`cao$checkSensitivity()`, `checkSensitivity()`, `checkCompositionSensitivity()`, `sensitivityFormulas()`): the alternative models were built from the fixed part of the formula, so a grouping term `(1 | g)` was missing from their formulas and labels, the composition alternatives lost it, and a screened grouping variable was proposed as an added covariate ("plus cohort"). The grouping terms are now kept in every alternative model, and a model without each grouping term ("minus (1 | g)") is added, which shows what the result would be if the grouping were ignored.
- Expression shifts per cell type (gsd), all relabelings enumerated: the smallest attainable p-value (`p.floor`) and the number of distinct relabelings (`n.perm.distinct`) were those of the cell type's own relabeling plan. The p-value is counted among the global relabelings, under which a sample present in the cell type takes the design row of the sample it is mapped to, also when that sample lacks the cell type; with samples missing from a cell type the two counts differ (SCC: Fibroblast p 1/384 below a reported floor of 1/320). Both are now counted among the relabelings the p-value is computed from.
- Notes of the cell types' own relabeling plans (e.g. "block permutation with continuous covariate(s) age is not exact") are printed once with the cell types that share them, not once per cell type.
- Analyses on a subset of the samples (a model on fewer samples than the object, e.g. after `excludeSamples()` or with missing covariate values): `estimateCellLoadings()` stopped with "Row counts of design matrix and ILR matrix do not match" and `estimateCellDensity()` with "subscript out of bounds"; both now use the model's samples. `excludeSamples()` removes the stored model with a message when its tested variable has fewer than two levels among the remaining samples (it stopped). `plotCodaSamples()` takes the tested variable from the test for a numeric tested variable (it stopped).
- Distance model, test of a dispersion change (`p.var`) in paired designs with the pairing variable fitted as a factor (`~ condition + patient`, `~ condition + x + patient`): with two samples per patient the two residuals of a patient are mirror images, so both samples have the same residual distance and a patient holding both conditions says nothing about their dispersions; the dispersion contrast then rests on the patients whose condition never changes under relabeling, and its p-value was arbitrary (partly paired null data: 8.0-12.6 % at p <= 0.05 with the design rows exchanged). The test is now not reported in that case (`p.var`, `var`, `total` NA, flag `dispersion-not-identifiable`, a note; `dispersionRandomizable()`); with the pairing variable as a grouping term it is tested as before.
- Model report: the warning that the tested variable is collinear with the other covariates measured its independent variation against its uncentered size, so a variable far from zero (an age) was reported as collinear even with no covariates, and an unbalanced two-level variable looked more collinear than it is. The variation is now measured around the mean, the message gives the share of the variance that is independent of the other covariates, and a note is added when it is below a quarter (warning below 9 %, as before).
- Cluster-free maps with `scale = "auto"` (expression shifts and density): the scale with the most called cells is reported, so the map had calls whenever any of the three scales had them. In paired data without any effect the expression-shift map called cells in 24-30 % of data sets (about 10 % at each single scale); in unpaired data and in the density map the scales err together and the rate was at 10 %. The choice is now tested by relabeling: the number of cells called at the chosen scale is compared with the same number for every relabeled data set (each put through all scales and the same rule; `scanCallCounts()`, `autoScaleTest()`), and the adjusted p of a cell is not below the p-value of that comparison (`scales$auto`). The gsd statistic now standardizes over the observed and the relabeled data sets alike (before, the observed value was standardized by the relabeled ones, and each relabeled value by a set that included itself, which made the observed data set slightly more extreme; `options(cacoa.gsd.symmetric = FALSE)` restores it). 400 paired data sets without an effect: calls in 10.0 % with both changes, 13.8 % with the test only, 30.2 % before. The cell-type gsd test moved by at most one percentage point in the designs examined. Of the 44 data sets with a label effect (in all cells or in one cell type) that had calls, one lost them. `options(cacoa.auto.scale.test = FALSE)` switches the test off.
- Distance model of expression shifts under the exchange of residuals: its shift F had a milder form of the loss described in the next entry (paired data: 59 % of cell types detected against 70 % when the rows of the design are exchanged; MS batches 35 % against 50 %), for the same reason: the kernel's F of a relabeled data set is right on average over the relabelings only. `flShiftF()` computes the F of every relabeling on the scale of the observed one (contrast variance and residual degrees of freedom of that relabeling, residuals independent of the relabeled contrast estimate); paired data 64 %, MS batches 47 %, without a tested effect 0.4-6.4 % of cell types at p <= 0.05 in the eight designs examined (before: 0.0-4.9 %). The shift, the dispersion statistics and the null bands are unchanged. The scale is set for both tests of expression shifts by `options(cacoa.residual.scale = "independent" | "relabeling" | "average")`. The weighted and robust fits, the tests of a whole factor and the covariate screen keep the average factor.
- Expression shifts (gsd) under the exchange of residuals (a continuous covariate in the model without a grouping term, e.g. `~ condition + age + batch`): the test had lost most of its sensitivity (paired data, 26 % reduction in a tenth of the genes: 31 % of cell types detected against 64 % when the rows of the design are exchanged; MS batches, 39 % reduction: 15 % against 37 %). Cause: the exchanged residuals are projections of the data, so the variance of the relabeled contrast estimate and the expected residual sum of squares differ between relabelings, by factors common to all genes. They were corrected by one average factor; what remained explained 87-98 % of the spread of the relabeled statistics and widened the reference distribution of a statistic that sums over thousands of genes four- to five-fold. Both quantities are now taken from the noise model for every relabeling and gene, and the residuals of the relabeled fit are made independent of the relabeled contrast estimate (kernel `test_genes_lm()`, `fl_norm = 3`). The observed statistic is unchanged, and so is the test wherever nothing is lost to the projection (a fitted factor alone). Without a tested effect 1.3-4.7 % of cell types have p <= 0.05 in the seven designs examined; the paired example is detected in 50 %, the MS example in 27 %. `options(cacoa.residual.scale = "relabeling")` omits the last step: more sensitive (66 % and 44 %) but 3.2-8.0 % without an effect, above 5 % where much of the residual space is lost (covariate related to the tested variable, several covariates, small batches); `"average"` restores the single factor, and stays the default when residuals leave the levels of a fitted factor (`options(cacoa.residuals.within = FALSE)`).
- Expression shifts: the standard error behind the interval of a shift was the delete-one jackknife, which is about 1.5 times too large when the shift is absent or weak (the shift is a squared distance; the jackknife counts the second-order part of its variance twice), so that significant cell types were drawn with intervals covering zero. The second-order part is now counted once: `se^2 = (jackknife^2 + v1) / 2`, with `v1` the first-order variance estimated from the residuals of the fit (`squaredDistanceLinearVar()`, `correctedJackknifeSE()`; distance model and per-gene magnitudes). In simulations with 4-10 samples per group the nominal 95 % interval covers 90-98 % (plain jackknife 96-100 %). The leave-one-sample-out range is unchanged; the plain jackknife error is kept as `influence$se.jackknife`.
- Composition loadings carry model-based standard errors and confidence intervals (least squares on the log ratios; `contrast$loadings$se`, `se.ref`, `df`). `plotCodaLoadings(interval = TRUE)` draws them; `min.effect` marks a fold change by dotted lines; `codaLoadingsTable(res, min.effect)` returns loadings, fold changes, intervals, p-values, whether the interval lies beyond the fold change, the number of cells behind each loading and its dependence on the pseudocount. The test remains the permutation test.
- Pseudocount of the composition model: default 0.5 cells in a sample of median size, scaled with the sample's cell number (`zero.pseudocount = 0.5`, `scale.pseudocount = TRUE`; previously 0.1 cells in every sample). In simulations from the example cohorts the loadings of rare cell types were overstated by 0.2-0.4 log2 at 0.1 and least biased at 0.25-0.5; significance was not affected by the choice. Each result records how far every loading moves when the pseudocount is halved or doubled (`pseudocount.shift`, in standard errors).
- `plotCodaSamples()` / `cao$plotCodaSamples()`: per cell type the samples' proportions by level of the tested variable, the fitted proportions with their intervals, samples without the cell type as open points, and the samples that carry the result named (omission changes the loading by more than one standard error, or residual beyond 3); `pair.by` joins samples of the same patient. `codaSampleDiagnostics()` returns the underlying table (residuals, exact leave-one-out influence).
- `cao$plotFitDiagnostics(analysis)`: one layout for the fit diagnostics of the composition, the expression shifts and the density map: samples by cell types, left the distance of each sample from the model's fit, right the change of the effect when the sample is left out (`plotSampleHeatmap()`, `shiftSampleResiduals()`).
- `cao$excludeSamples(samples)`: leaves samples out of every analysis run afterwards (their cells become unassigned, the model is rebuilt); `cao$excludeSamples(NULL)` restores them. Nothing is excluded automatically.
- Composition test with covariates: the cell types were tested by the unstudentized loading, which is not a valid statistic under residual permutation when the model has covariates (with a patient effect in SCC-like cohorts, 17 samples and 11 parameters, 21 % of unchanged cell types had p < 0.05 and 43 % of null cohorts a false call; the global test rejected in 57 %). They are now tested by the studentized loading recomputed on every relabeled data set (`contrastPermutationTest()`; 4 % in the same simulations), the global test is the sum of the squared t statistics, and the null band of the loadings plot, the reference block and the contrast tree use the relabeled t statistics at the observed standard errors. Models without covariates are unaffected in validity; their p-values change slightly. `settings$per.celltype.statistic` and `contrast$global$statistic` record what was used.
- Density map with discrete covariates: `estimateDiffCellDensity(perm.method = "auto")` (new default) takes the relabeling scheme of the model's test: sample labels are relabeled within the levels of discrete covariates and grouping variables when every adjusting covariate is discrete, and residual permutation is used only with continuous covariates. Residual permutation across all samples with a covariate of many levels (a patient term) is not valid for the density map: on data sets without a label effect (cells of each sample split into two pseudo-samples, the sample as covariate) it called cells at 10 % FDR in 67-78 % of the data sets, relabeling within the levels in 0-5 %.
- Seurat objects: the counts and the normalized values are read through `seuratAssayData()`, which names the layer by the argument the installed SeuratObject understands (`layer` from version 5, `slot` before). `GetAssayData(layer = "counts")` on SeuratObject 4 ignores `layer` with a warning and returns the normalized values, so every analysis that reads counts (expression shifts by gsd and by the distance model, cluster-free expression shifts, differential expression per cell type) was computed from log-normalized values on such installations: in `extractJointCountMatrix()` since the Seurat 5 compatibility change of 2025-08, and in `extractRawCountMatrices()` in the development version of 2026-11 (before that, `object[[assay]]$counts` failed for the classic assay class). A counts layer that is empty is an error, and one with values that are not whole numbers gives a warning (`tests/testthat/test-seurat-access.R`).
- Grouping term with a tested variable that is constant within its levels (a condition per donor, several samples per donor): `~ condition + (1 | donor)` is now tested by every relabeling-based analysis (it was an error). The levels of the grouping variable that hold one value of the tested variable are exchanged as wholes: among levels with the same make-up (the same number of samples in every cell of the model's other discrete covariates) the j-th sample of a level takes the design row of the j-th sample of another, so that the label and every covariate move with the level and the levels are the units of the test (`permutationPlan(whole.levels = TRUE)`, `wholeUnitCells()`; `plan$unit`, `plan$slot`). Levels that hold both values are relabeled within, as before; a level with a make-up of its own keeps its value. For samples absent from a cell type or a neighborhood the levels are regrouped by the make-up of their present samples (`inducePermutation(plan = )`, `induced_perm()` in the compiled fitter, which takes `perm_units` / `perm_slots`), and a level never receives the rows of two levels. Consequences: with one sample per level a grouping term changes nothing; in partly paired designs (SCC: paired patients and patients with one sample) the levels with one value are no longer fixed when levels of the same make-up hold different values; one grouping variable only. When nothing can be exchanged the analyses stop with a message that says so. `estimateDEPerCellType()` (p-values from the model) cannot use this and stops for such a design. In a simulation with a donor effect (two samples per donor, no effect of the condition) relabeling single samples gave 15-16 % of p <= 0.05, the exchange of whole donors 3-4 % (`tests/testthat/test-whole-levels.R`).
- Exchange of residuals (Freedman-Lane) with discrete covariates in the model: the residuals are exchanged within the levels of the discrete covariates (and of `block.vars`), as the tested variable is when it is exchanged; previously they were exchanged among all samples. The rule for every test is now: discrete covariates and grouping terms define the cells within which the exchange happens; a continuous covariate decides that residuals are exchanged (without a grouping term) or that the tested variable and the covariate are exchanged together (with one). For `~ tested + x + pair` (a fitted factor with a level per pair of samples) the exchange among all samples was valid but found an added expression difference in 16 % of the cell-type tests, against 31 % within the pairs (gsd; distance model 15 % against 59 %), with 1.6-2.8 % false positives either way. `permutationPlan(residuals.within = FALSE)` or `options(cacoa.residuals.within = FALSE)` gives the former behavior, for models whose levels are too small to leave enough relabelings (`tests/testthat/test-residual-strata.R`).
- Differential expression with DESeq2 (`estimateDEPerCellType(test = "DESeq2.Wald")`, the default, and `"DESeq2.LRT"`): the dispersions were estimated with the design `~ 1` and the model matrix was passed to the test only (`estimateDEForTypeDESeq()`, since the model-object change of 2025-12), so that the differences explained by the model (the tested variable, covariates, a patient factor) were counted as dispersion. The dispersions are now estimated with the model matrix, which makes the result identical to `DESeq2::DESeq()` with the same design (`tests/testthat/test-de-deseq.R`). On the MS example (9 control and 12 MS samples, 14 cell types, `~ diagnosis`) the number of genes at 5 % FDR goes from 130 to 1,391; on SCC (`~ condition`) from 3,765 to 15,548. On data without a label effect (ALZ, 6 against 6 samples) the corrected test has a gene at 5 % FDR in 13 % of the cell-type analyses (0 % before, when it had almost no power), comparable to edgeR (20 %) and limma-voom (11 %); with a fitted pair factor 0.8 %.
- Distance model of expression shifts under the exchange of residuals: the relabeled Gower matrix is built from residuals multiplied by sqrt(kappa) (`flKappa()`, `flGowerParts(kappa)`), so that the unstudentized shift, the total and the null bands of the relabeled data sets are on the scale of the observed ones; the pseudo-F of the shift is a ratio and does not change.
- `gsdShiftsPerCellType(return.profiles = TRUE)` returns the per-cell-type profiles the gsd test is computed from (for evaluations of the statistic outside the compiled kernel).
- p-values when all distinct relabelings are enumerated (small designs, e.g. 128 relabelings within 7 patient pairs): the per-column fitter and the composition test counted the observed labeling twice, `(k + 1) / (B + 1)`, although it is one of the enumerated relabelings. They now use `k / B`, as expression shifts and the shift map already did (`relabelingPValue()`); the smallest attainable p-value is halved (SCC composition, relabeling within patients: 6 cell types at 5 % FDR instead of none).
- One relabeling rule for all tests. The composition (`estimateCellLoadings()`) and cluster-free differential expression (`estimateClusterFreeDE()`) take their relabeling scheme from the model's test (`perm.method = "auto"`, the new default), as expression shifts, the shift map and the density map do: the tested variable is exchanged within the levels of discrete covariates and grouping terms; residuals are exchanged when the model has a continuous covariate. Both previously exchanged residuals among all samples for every model.
- Grouping term in the composition: `(1 | g)` now only restricts the relabeling to within the levels of `g`, as in every other test. The random baseline per level is an option, `estimateCellLoadings(random.baseline = TRUE)` (previously fitted whenever the formula had a grouping term). A design in which every level of `g` has one value of the tested variable is an error for every test unless the random baseline is requested.
- Exchange of residuals (Freedman-Lane), per-column fitter (density map, cluster-free differential expression, composition): the full model, nuisance terms included, is now fitted to every relabeled data set. Previously the exchanged residuals were regressed on the residualized tested column only, which made the t statistic of the relabeled data sets too small when the model without the tested variable had many parameters relative to samples (a covariate with a level per patient: 21-25 % false positives at a nominal 5 % in the composition coordinates, cells called in 49-80 % of null data sets by the density map; 4-6 % after the change).
- Exchange of residuals, scale: the residuals of the model without the tested variable are smaller than the errors. They are multiplied by sqrt(kappa), kappa = the variance of the observed over the mean variance of the relabeled contrast estimate, computed from the design and the relabelings (1 when the exchange stays within the levels of the fitted terms, about n / (n - q) for a free exchange with q parameters). This matters for statistics with a part that is not recomputed from the relabeled data: the moderated t of the density map, the noise-model variance of gsd (expression shifts with a patient covariate under residual exchange: 21-31 % false positives before), an unstudentized loading (17-18 % before). In the gsd kernel the model variance of the relabeled data sets is divided by kappa.
- `contrastPermutationTest(scheme = "block")`: the composition's t test exchanges the rows of the design when the model's scheme is the exchange of the tested variable (it exchanged residuals for either scheme).
- Default relabeling scheme with a continuous covariate and a grouping term (`~ tested + x + (1 | g)`): the rows of the design are exchanged within the levels of `g` (scheme `block`); previously residuals were exchanged within the levels. With groups that are not fitted, the covariate's coefficient in the model without the tested variable is fitted partly to the group baselines, and the exchanged residuals are not comparable with the observed ones (up to 10-11 % false positives at a nominal 5 % for expression shifts, composition and the density map; 4-7 % with the exchange of rows). A model with a continuous covariate and no grouping term keeps the exchange of residuals. Forcing `permutation = "freedman-lane"` on such a model adds a note.
- Model report (`setModel()`, `print()` of the model): the grouping terms, how many samples can change their value under relabeling and the smallest attainable p-value are printed with each test. A grouping variable with levels that hold one value of the tested variable gives a note with the number of samples that inform the test. A tested variable with one value in every level gives a warning from `setModel()` and an error from every analysis (the composition with `random.baseline = TRUE` excepted). The same variable as a covariate and as a grouping term is an error.
- `estimateDEPerCellType()` with a grouping term: DESeq2, edgeR and limma take their p-values from the model and cannot use a grouping term; it is fitted as a fixed term with a warning, and covariates that are constant within its levels are dropped and named in the warning (`groupingAsFixedFormula()`).
- `estimateDiffCellDensity()` tests with the current model, as every other analysis (it used the model stored with the density estimate); a message says so when the two differ.
- Grouping terms in the model formula: `~ condition + (1 | patient)`. The term is taken out of the fixed formula; where the tested variable varies within the levels of the grouping variable, sample labels are relabeled within those levels in every analysis (as `block.vars`). The composition model in addition fits a random intercept per level (variance ratio by restricted maximum likelihood per cell type, generalized least squares) and tests the loadings against relabeled data sets on which the whole fit, including the variance ratio, is repeated (`randomInterceptContrast()`); this also covers designs in which the tested variable takes one value per level (several samples per donor), which the other analyses do not yet test. Simulated type I error 3-6 % at a nominal 5 % for paired, partly confounded and between-group designs, with more power than a fixed effect or relabeling within levels alone.

## Composition

- Contrast tree of the compositional result (`cao$plotContrastTree()`, `contrastTree()`, `plotContrastTree()`): a sequential binary partition of the cell types that explains the contrast's compositional shift with the fewest balances, built from the contrast's clr loadings and their permutation null without refitting anything. The contrast's effect on a balance is the difference of the mean loadings of its two sides (times `sqrt(n1 n2 / (n1 + n2))`), and over the `D - 1` balances of any such tree the squared effects add up to the contrast's variance explained in ILR space, so the tree is built top-down by the split with the largest effect (a contiguous split of the loadings in sorted order; `split = "evidence"` takes the largest effect over its null sd). Each node reports the log2 ratio of the geometric-mean proportions of its sides between the contrast's endpoints, an exact permutation p-value (the same relabelings as the per-cell-type test, covariates respected), BH over the nodes, and the share of the shift it explains. The plot shows the tree with the node labels, the samples' own ratios at the significant nodes (adjusted to the endpoints' covariate values; medians and quartiles per side of the contrast) and the reference-centered loadings under the leaves. `lmCoda()` stores the tree (`tree`). The former score-based tree (`constructTreeScore()`, `plotContrastTreeScore()`, `getCodaContrastScore()`) is gone; `cao$plotCodaContrastTree()` now draws the new tree.
- Reference cell types for the presentation of the loadings (`ref.method = "block"`, the default of `estimateCellLoadings()` / `lmCoda()`; `referenceBlock()`): the largest set of cell types whose proportions do not change relative to each other, found by merging cell types whose loading differences are not distinguishable from their permutation null (pairwise tests on the same relabelings, merged while p >= `ref.block.thresh = 0.05`; among blocks within a factor of two of the largest, which the composition cannot tell apart, the one closest to the center of the composition). The previous rule (cell types with the weakest evidence of a change relative to the geometric mean, p > 0.3, one to three of them; `ref.method = "pvalue"`) centered the loadings on cell types near the clr center and could leave unchanged cell types looking changed: with one cell type doubled and five unchanged, it picked the one unchanged type that happened to sit at the center and four of the unchanged types kept adjusted p < 0.05; the block rule finds the five. Each cell type also gets a p-value relative to the reference (`per_cell$pval.ref`, `padj.ref`: the change `l_c - mean(l over the reference)` against its permutation null), available in the plots as `significance = "reference"`; the filled points and stars of every plot use the primary, reference-free test (relative to the geometric mean; `pval` / `padj`) by default, since the reference is chosen from the data and can change with the relabelings drawn.
- `plotCellLoadings()` / `plotCodaLoadings()`: the axis is log2 (it was the natural log while the tree and bar plots were log2), `center = "reference"` (default) or `"none"` (the loadings as they are, zero = the geometric mean of all cell types), with the other frame's zero marked by a dashed line (the geometric mean on the reference-centered axis, the reference block on the other), and `cao$plotCellLoadings(center = "both")` draws the two side by side; the band is the null of the test behind the points placed on the same axis.
- Predicted endpoint compositions come with intervals (`lmCoda(ci.level = 0.95)`: `predicted$baseline.lo` / `.hi`, `target.lo` / `.hi`, from the ILR fit: the fitted endpoint coordinates with their covariance, multivariate t draws mapped to proportions), drawn as error bars by `plotCodaCellTypes(value = "predicted", show.ci = TRUE)`; the fold-change caption line is gone.
- `cao$plotCodaCellTypes()` (predicted baseline / target proportions, reference-centered effects, volcano), `cao$plotCodaGlobal()` and `cao$plotCodaHeatmap()` refused every `lmCoda()` result (they tested for a `coefficients` element that `lmCoda()` does not return); fixed. The stars of every `plotCodaCellTypes()` view and the filled points of `plotCellLoadings()` are the same test (the primary one, relative to the geometric mean; `significance = "reference"` for the test relative to the reference cell types), and the predicted-proportion plot states the offset between a proportion's fold change and the loading: the change of the geometric mean of the proportions, the same for every cell type (MS +0.78 log2: the loss of the neurons raises every other share by 1.7x, so an oligodendrocyte loading of +0.6 relative to the glial block appears as a 3.3x proportion).

## Sensitivity

- `cao$checkSensitivity(analysis = "composition")` (`checkCompositionSensitivity()`): the compositional contrast refitted under the alternative covariate sets of the expression-shift sensitivity, with the loadings (log2, relative to the geometric mean) and their permutation tests; the same verdicts per cell type, stored as "composition.sensitivity" and plotted by `cao$plotSensitivity(analysis = "composition")`.
- `checkSensitivity(jackknife = TRUE)` refits every alternative model with each sample left out (both analyses), which gives each estimate its jackknife standard error and leave-one-out range; `plotSensitivity(interval = "jackknife")` (default) draws the 95 % jackknife interval and the leave-one-out range (thin line) for every model that has them, `interval = "none"` the points only. `lmCoda()` results record their `settings`.

## Statistics

- The moderated permutation statistic fits its scaled-F prior itself (`fitFPrior()`: the moments of the log variances, a lowess trend on the column mean), so the trended moderation of the kernel-density map no longer needs limma and no longer disappears silently when limma is absent. The estimated prior degrees of freedom are capped (`estimateDiffCellDensity(moderate.d0.max = 4)`; `performLMPermutations(moderate.d0.max)`, no cap by default there): on the PF cohort the fit returns an infinite prior df, which replaced every cell's own variance by the trend and cost the cells with a real difference a fifth of their statistic (kNN units: 964 cells at FDR 10 % uncapped, 3,301 with the cap, 3,510 unmoderated).

## Cluster-free analyses

- One `scale` argument sets the neighbourhoods of both cluster-free maps (`estimateDiffCellDensity(scale)`, `estimateClusterFreeExpressionShifts(scale)`): diffusion neighbourhoods on the cell graph whose width is the share of the cells a typical neighbourhood covers (the kernel strength solved per graph, `solveKernelBeta()`; for the shift map a neighborhood is the scale's number of cells that its centre's heat reaches most, `diffusionNeighbourhoodLists()`: the centres are cells in no neighborhood yet whose own kernel reaches at least `min.reach` of the scale's cells, only their kernels are computed, and afterwards every cell joins, up to `cover` neighborhoods, the centres reaching it with at least half their boundary weight (the kernel is symmetric), a cell in no neighborhood the centre reaching it most, so that the weakly connected cells (whose kernel stays near themselves) are tested in their neighbors' neighborhoods; every neighborhood is at least the kNN unit size `k`, so that it holds enough cells per sample to be tested). `scale = "auto"` (the default) runs the scale scan over `auto.scales` (1, 3 and 5 % of the cells for both maps; for the shift map 10 % as the coarsest scale is ruled out because its scale-sized neighbourhoods take in the halo around a changed population and call it: on the benchmark the false share at q < 0.1 rose to 0.12 on small changed populations against 0.006 with 5 %, at the same recall; the density map, whose kernel neighborhoods are soft, had 1, 3 and 10 % until 2026-10-29: on the 480 figure sets 1/3/5 costs it 0.007 ranking recall (MS 0.732 vs 0.744, PF 0.415 vs 0.423, SCC 0.306 vs 0.308) and 0.012 recall at q < 0.1 while lowering PF's false share at q < 0.1 from 0.052 to 0.016, and one shared set was preferred) and reports the single scale whose own map calls the most cells (the finest of the scales calling nearly all cells when there are such), so that the q-values and effects of the map belong to one scale; a single value tests that scale; several values report the scan itself (`scanDensityScales()`, `scanShiftScales()`): every scale under the same relabelings, a cell's statistic its smallest p over the scales, its q-value the pooled permutation false discovery rate of that statistic (calibrated against the same quantity under every relabeling; no Bonferroni factor), the reported effect the finest scale's (`effect.scale`). Each scale's own map is kept (`scales`). `scale = NULL` keeps the kNN units. The density map's two-map union rule (`broad`, `combineDensityMaps()`) is gone, replaced by the scan.
- `clusterFreeExpressionShifts(centres)` tests the neighbourhoods of given centres (each scattered to its cells); the transform scale of the normalized profiles is `profile.scale` (was `scale`).
- `heatSmooth()` applies the Chebyshev recurrence to blocks of columns in parallel (`n.cores`), which the kernel strength search, the per-sample densities and the neighborhood construction all go through.
- The density map counts each cell as one cell of its diffusion neighborhood (`ownCellDensity()`, used by `heatLogDensity()` and `estimateCellDensity(smoothing = "heat")`): at a cell the per-sample densities are the heat arriving from each sample's cells, rescaled to the effective number of cells it represents, plus one cell of the cell's own sample. On the unweighted kNN graphs of the benchmark this is the map as before (the cell's own heat is one cell's share there). On a Conos graph the degree is the number of mutual matches, small for the cells of a population that is abundant in its own sample, and such a cell keeps most of its heat at one global strength; counted at the kernel's weight, its own sample stood alone at the cell whatever the labels, while the heat that did arrive carried the composition of the neighborhood it is matched to. With the change the poorly matched cells are tested through that neighborhood: on the benchmark's MS depletion sets the recall at q < 0.1 in the three most poorly matched degree deciles rises from 0.37 / 0.50 / 0.58 to 0.63 / 0.67 / 0.69 (a Harmony kNN graph of the same cells: 0.70 / 0.68 / 0.67), the false share, the null sets and the 200-relabeling calibration on the Conos graphs are unchanged, and no cost is added (the kernel's diagonal and the arriving heat's effective size come from the probe vectors of the strength search, `kernelEffectiveSize()`). Two limits remain: the effect at a poorly matched cell is that of the neighborhood it borrows, and per-type sums of the map's densities are not composition estimates (the composition tests are).
- The kNN neighborhood of the cell graph (`scale = NULL`; `estimateCellDensity(smoothing = "knn")`, `estimateClusterFreeExpressionShifts(k)`), sized by `defaultNeighbourhoodSize()`, remains available and is the smallest neighborhood of the shift map's diffusion neighborhoods; the heat kernel with its width set in cells remains available for the density (`smoothing = "heat"`, `target.size`).
- Both maps average the per-cell statistic over the cell and its graph neighbours under every relabeling (`post = "averaged"`, the default; `"inherit"` and `"none"` as options) and adjust across cells with the pooled permutation false-discovery rate of the standardized (shifts) or studentized (density) statistic; `pooled.statistic` switches between the two.
- Unadjusted z-scores are the signed tail z of the pooled permutation p-value (`pooled` / `z.pooled`), adjusted z-scores the same transform of the q-values; `plotDiffCellDensity(what = "effect")` shows the effect.
- The density map's message now counts the cells at FDR 10 % (`q < 0.1`); it counted the cells with |adjusted z| >= 1.28, which for this two-sided map is FDR 20 % (the one-sided shift map's 1.28 is FDR 10 %).
- `plotDiffCellDensity(outline = TRUE)` and `plotClusterFreeExpressionShifts(outline = TRUE)` draw a thin outline around the regions where the significant cells are (`significanceContours()`: the local fraction of cells with adjusted p below `outline.alpha` among all cells, from two-dimensional kernel densities on the embedding, contoured where it reaches `outline.level`, 0.5 by default; specks are dropped), so that the significant regions can be read whatever the colour scale shows.
- `plotClusterFreeExpressionShifts(z.floor = qnorm(0.99))`: the colour range of the z-score panels reaches at least 2.33, so that the colours saturate only beyond p = 0.01 (it reached 1.96 on the adjusted and the data's own quantile on the unadjusted panel, and a map whose z-scores barely reach significance looked uniformly saturated).
- The per-cell kernels run in C++ with one shared accumulator (no permutations x cells matrix in memory), a closed-form fitter for the default density case and a two-group closed form for the permuted shift statistic.
- `cao$checkNeighbourhoods()` reports unit sizes, cells per sample per unit and purity against the annotation for a neighbourhood size.
- The expression-shift map uses normalized, noise-weighted neighbourhood profiles by default (`profile = "normalized"`): per unit and sample, summed counts over the summed full library sizes, log10(scale p + 1) with `scale` the median library size, genes weighted by 1 / (sampling variance + between-sample floor) from a label-free noise model (`fitShiftNoiseCurves()`), and `dist = "cor"` now centres genes across the unit's samples (`"euclidean"` as an alternative). The previous raw mean-count profiles remain available as `profile = "raw"`.
- The expression-shift map tests each neighbourhood with a per-gene statistic by default (`statistic = "gsd"`): per gene the contrast estimate over its variance (the noise model's prediction moderated towards the residual variance of each relabeled fit), combined over genes adaptively (the maximum over gamma = 1, 2, 4, 8 and the max of the standardized log sum of |z|^gamma). It handles any single-df contrast (two groups, numeric variables, covariates; block relabeling or Freedman-Lane). Genes are chosen per neighbourhood (`neighbourhood.gene.frac`, default: detected in 5 % of the neighbourhood's cells, from the genes detected in 1 % of all cells), and only the neighbourhoods of a subset of cells are tested (`centres.per.cell`, default about 2 centres among each cell's graph neighbours; each cell averages the tested neighbourhoods that contain it, and the per-cell test stays exact), and cells are called by BH on the pooled p. `robust = "winsor"` winsorizes each gene's values within the neighbourhood (label-free). The sample-distance pseudo-F remains available as `statistic = "distance"` with its previous defaults.
- Cell-type expression shifts (`estimateExpressionShiftMagnitudes()`) test the shift with the per-gene statistic gsd by default (`statistic = "gsd"`, also `gsdShiftsPerCellType()`): each cell type tested as one group of cells with the statistic of the cluster-free map; exact permutation p (relabelings enumerated when there are fewer than `n.permutations`, each type reporting its own number of distinct relabelings and p floor), BH and max-statistic across types; per-gene changes and z-scores per cell type in `genes`. At least 5 cells per sample and 2 samples per compared level by default; the per-gene variance is moderated towards the model variance with `gsd.d0 = 3` prior degrees of freedom (was 10: broad weak changes lost power, e.g. SCC 1,000 genes at 1.23x 0.35 -> 0.50 at p < 0.05, calibration unchanged); the cluster-free map uses the same default (neutral on its benchmark: recall 0.81 / 0.46 with either, calibration unchanged). `gsd.d0` is an argument of both methods. The effect sizes come from the pairwise distance model on the samples with at least `min.cells.magnitude` (10) cells (profiles of fewer cells are dominated by sampling noise, which shrinks the cosine shift: with 5 cells, MS EN-PYR 0.22 -> 0.08; a type tested by gsd without enough such samples is reported and plotted without a magnitude): the normalized shift (distance between the condition means over the within-group dispersions), the dispersion change `var` (log2 ratio of the dispersions, tested by the distance model) and `total`, with jackknife errors and influence; the distance test's own shift p-value is kept (`p.shift.distance`). `statistic = "distance"` gives the previous distance-only results. With too few expressed genes for the gsd noise model the distance test is used, with a warning.
- Expression shifts are estimated for contrasts (two levels, a combination of levels, a numeric variable, with covariates); whole-factor tests of the model (used by DE, composition and the covariate screen) are skipped, and requested alone they are an error.
- The dispersion design of the distance model includes a per-sample 1 / (number of cells) term (`cell.number.adjust = TRUE`): the dispersion change is taken at equal cell numbers. Without it a condition with fewer cells per sample appeared more dispersed (null data with one condition's cells thinned to 30 %: false positives of `var` 7.8 % -> 14.9 % on MS; in simulation 73 % without and 8 % with the term).
- The dispersion change is tested with a studentized statistic (the change over its standard error from the dispersion fit, `var.t` in the permutation statistics): its precision depends on how strongly cell numbers and the tested variable are correlated, which differs between the observed labels and a relabeling. In simulation with one condition having about 3x fewer cells: false positives of `var` 4 % (68 % without the cell-number term).
- Printed and plotted p-values just below the significance level are shown with one more digit (0.0497, not 0.05), and the printed count of significant cell types uses the unrounded values (it could disagree with the fill of the plot and with the progress message).
- The results record how strongly the cell numbers are tied to the contrast (`cell.assoc`, |correlation| of log cells with the per-sample contrast weights) and flag `cells-tied-to-contrast` above 0.5; the dispersion plot marks such cell types. Null checks (MS, one condition's cells thinned to 30 %): the `var` test is calibrated where the association is below 0.3 (5 %), mildly liberal at 0.3-0.5 (9 %) and anticonservative above 0.5 (18 %).
- `plotExpressionShiftGenes()`: the genes with the largest per-gene z-scores of the shift test in the significant cell types (genes changed at gene-level FDR `gene.fdr` as dots sized by |z| and coloured by the log2 fold change, the others as small grey dots).
- `plotCellLoadings()` / `plotCodaLoadings()` follow the style of the shift plot: ordered by the loading (`ordering = "loading"`, the default), the permutation null as a grey 95 % band, filled points when significant, adjusted p-values at the right, reference cell types marked "(ref)" (the -log10 p panel and the caption are gone).
- `plotClusterFreeExpressionShifts()`: the maps keep the aspect ratio of the embedding, legends below; `ncol` sets the layout.
- Under relabeling, the columns of the dispersion design that are properties of the sample (nuisance covariates of `dispersion.formula`, the cell-number term) stay with the sample; only the columns involving the tested variable are relabeled (C++ kernels and the R reference).
- Each cell type's results carry the 2.5-97.5 % range of the normalized shift and of the dispersion change over relabelings (`null.shift.lo/hi`, `null.var.lo/hi`; the half of the relabelings least associated with the tested contrast, so that a real effect does not leak into the band).
- `plotExpressionShiftMagnitudes(effect = c("shift", "var"))`: one row per cell type ordered by the size of the effect, cell-type colours, filled when significant (the shift by gsd), the relabeling band (`show.null`), adjusted p-values at the right (`show.pvalues`); optional jackknife intervals (`show.ci`) and a margin with the number of genes changed at a gene-level FDR (`show.genes`, `gene.fdr`; power-dependent, a cross-check). `checkSensitivity()` and `plotShiftDetail()` work on gsd results (distance-model estimates).
- Gene filters count a gene as detected in a cell when its count is above 0. The per-cell-type DE filter (`getPerCellTypeGeneFilter()`, `min.cell.frac`) and the gene list of the cluster-based expression shifts (`pseudobulkPerCellType()`, `min.gene.frac`) required a count above 1, so genes detected mostly as single UMIs were dropped (about a third to half of the genes of the shift gene list on the example datasets). Gene universes, DE results, shift magnitudes and their tests change accordingly.
- Cluster-based expression shifts use the same profiles as the cluster-free map: pseudobulk counts normalized by the full library sizes of the (cell type, sample) cells, `log10(scale * count / N + 1)` with `scale` the median per-cell library size (`pseudobulkPerCellType(normalization = "library")`); the previous normalization by the total over the selected genes is `normalization = "selected"`.
- `-fno-math-errno` in `PKG_CXXFLAGS` (math functions need not set `errno`; results unchanged) lets the per-gene loops vectorize.

## One gene set per cell type and per neighborhood

- Every per-unit analysis now uses one rule: a cell type (or, in the cluster-free maps, a neighborhood) is analyzed on the genes of the common list detected in at least 5 % of its cells (pooled over the samples). Previously the cell-type gsd test used this rule while the distance model behind the shift magnitudes, the covariate screen, the variance partition and the sample maps used the common list for every cell type, and the cluster-free distance map used the 3,000 most expressed genes. The magnitudes change by a few percent (their ranking across cell types and the distance test's power are unchanged on the benchmark sets); the cluster-free distance map gains region-specific changes (outside the global top genes) and loses some recall on cell-type-wide ones.
- New option `type.gene.frac` (default 0.05; `cao$setOptions()`), also an argument of `estimateExpressionShiftMagnitudes()` (`neighbourhood.gene.frac` there is its earlier name). `typeGeneSets()` returns the per-type gene sets; `pseudobulkPerCellType()` returns the per-type detection fractions (`det.frac`); the per-type distance matrices carry their gene count (`attr(D, "n.genes")`).
- `estimateClusterFreeExpressionShifts(statistic = "distance")`: `neighbourhood.gene.frac` now applies to the distance map too, and `n.top.genes` defaults to `NULL` (no restriction); `n.top.genes = 3000, neighbourhood.gene.frac = 0` gives the previous gene set.
- GSEA (`estimateOntology(type = "GSEA")`) no longer fails intermittently with "database disk image is malformed" when run on several cores: the organism is read from the annotation database once, before the workers are forked.

## Diffusion neighborhoods: why a share of the cells

- The diffusion follows the graph, so each cell's neighborhood stays mostly within its own population (at a median of 10 %, populations of 400 to 12,000 cells had neighborhoods of 0.5 to 2 times their own size), and when the benchmark data were downsampled to 50, 20 and 10 % of their cells the best width stayed at about 10 % of the cells, whereas the best fixed kernel strength and the best number of cells changed several-fold. A strong kernel is applied in steps of at most `beta = 120` (`heatSmooth()`; the polynomial approximation of a single strong step is poor), and `solveKernelBeta()` searches up to `beta = 5000`.
- The per-sample kernel densities are tested on `log2(d + kernel.alpha / n.cells)` of the densities normalized to sum 1 (`kernel.alpha = 0.3`; `heatLogDensity()`), with the permutation statistic moderated towards a prior variance trended on the cell's mean, estimated without the labels inside every relabeling (`moderate = TRUE`, needs limma).
- The heat kernel as the only unit (`estimateCellDensity(smoothing = "heat")`) is now tested on the same log scale by default (`transform = "auto"` gives `"log"`): the previous `"log"` added a pseudo-count of half a cell, which swamps a kernel density, and the linear default lost power on SCC and PF (`transform = "none"` keeps it).
- The moderated permutation statistic is available to the density test generally (`performLMPermutations(moderate.perm = "fixed" | "squeeze" | "trend")`, the closed-form fitter only): the prior is re-estimated label-free, so the per-cell test stays exact.

## Sensitivity analysis

- `checkSensitivity()` answers two questions per cell type and reports them separately: whether the size of the shift depends on the covariate set or on single samples (the distance-model magnitude, as in the main result: near zero (within the relabeling noise band of the main result), stable, changes by x%, sign depends on the model, driven by one sample; `verdict.magnitude`), and whether its significance does (`verdict.significance`). The significance is now that of the main result's test: with `statistic = "gsd"` the gsd test is refitted under every alternative model with the main result's settings and permutation count (`significance = "main"`, default); previously the alternative models, and the current one, were judged by the distance model's own test, so the current row could contradict the main result. `significance = "distance"` gives the previous behavior. The printout gives both verdicts, `plotSensitivity()` both in the panel titles and the test in the subtitle (each panel shows only the models fitted for that cell type); the combined `verdict` is kept.

## Plots

- `plotShiftDetail()`: the pair-distance panel shows the normalized shift, var and total of the results table (it showed the fit's unnormalized values under the same names).
- `plotExpressionShiftMagnitudes()`, `plotCellLoadings()`: the adjusted-p column is placed clear of the largest point; the gene-count side panel (`show.genes`) drops overlapping axis labels and says that grey bars are significant shifts.
- `plotCovariateScreen()`: the global p-values no longer overlap their significance dots; blank tiles (not tested) are explained. `plotCovariateSummary()` states when the partial mode fell back to marginal.

## Sample spaces and maps

- Sample distances are available in three spaces, with or without a model: `"expression"` (one cell type, or all cell types combined when `cell.type` is not given: the average of the per-type distances, each type weighted by its cells, `joint.weighting = "cells"`, or equally, `"equal"`), `"pseudobulk"` (one pseudobulk of all cells per sample, so composition differences enter the distance; previously listed but not implemented) and `"composition"` (Euclidean distance of the ILR coordinates of the cell-type counts, as in the covariate screen; previously the coordinates stored by `estimateCellLoadings()`). `getSampleDistanceMatrix()` and `plotSampleDistances()` take these names; `"expression.shifts"`, `"coda"` and `"pseudo.bulk"` still work.
- `plotSampleDistances()` no longer needs `estimateExpressionShiftMagnitudes()` or `estimateCellLoadings()` first (expression distances come from stored shift results when there are any, otherwise they are computed with the same defaults), so the maps can be drawn next to the covariate screen, before the model is chosen. Its subtitle gives the chance-corrected R² and permutation p-value of the `color.by` / `shape.by` variables on the distances shown (`show.r2`; the marginal screen of that one matrix); a covariate removed by the adjustment is marked as such. The default is now `values = "unadjusted"`; `"adjusted"` (and `"both"`) remove the model's covariates other than the tested variable, in every space. The MDS now embeds the square root of the correlation and L1 distances, which the distance models treat as squared distances (previously they were squared a second time, which exaggerated the large distances), so the map shows the geometry the R² describes. Solid point shapes, readable legend keys with `show.sample.size`.
- `screenCovariates()` adds summary rows for the combined sample spaces (`summary.rows`, default "all cell types" and "composition"; also "all cell types (equal weights)" and "pooled pseudobulk"). They are shown first in `plotCovariateScreen()`, BH-adjusted among themselves and kept out of the global p-values, the counts of significant cell types and the suggested model. The printout lists the covariates at FDR per summary row, naming the mode used (partial, or marginal where the partial mode fell back for lack of residual degrees of freedom). `space = "expression"` is the new name of `"expression.shifts"`.
- `estimateMetadataSeparation()` / `plotMetadataSeparation()` are deprecated: the "all cell types" row of the screen gives the same R². The unused arguments `show.pvalues` (now `show.r2`), and the description of `trim` / `k`, were removed or corrected.

## Permutations
